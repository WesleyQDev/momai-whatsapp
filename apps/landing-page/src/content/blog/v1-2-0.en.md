About a month ago, MomAI was at version 0.9.1. It worked, but I knew there was much more to be done. The Python backend loaded too many libraries, model initialization was slow, and the user experience was still far from what I envisioned for a local personal assistant.

So I started rewriting.

Version 1.2.0 is the result of approximately a month of intense work, 144 commits, and dozens of decisions about what to cut, what to keep, and what to rebuild.
![MomAI 1.2](/images/v1.2.0/captura01.png)
And yes, a large part of this development was accelerated by the use of artificial intelligence, especially open-source models that helped with review, refactoring, and even code generation. AI helping build local AI.

## Models updated to Qwen3.5

The most significant change is in the language models. The entire lineup was replaced by the **Qwen3.5** series, which brings important improvements in performance and response quality.

| Tier | Before (v0.9.1) | Now (v1.2.0) |
|------|----------------|---------------|
| Lite | Qwen3-0.6B | **Qwen3.5-0.8B** |
| Pro | LFM2.5-1.2B | **Qwen3.5-2B** |
| Ultra | Qwen3-4B | **Qwen3.5-4B** |

The Lite model jumped from 0.6B to 0.8B parameters, a 33% increase in raw capacity. Pro replaced the LFM2.5-1.2B, a little-known model with limited support, with **Qwen3.5-2B**, which delivers more natural and accurate responses. Ultra kept the 4B parameters but now with Qwen3.5 generation, more efficient and modern.

The Qwen3.5 benchmarks show consistent advances over the previous generation in reasoning, language understanding, and code generation tasks, with emphasis on computational efficiency: more performance per processed token.

One of the most common complaints in the previous version was the fixed context window. Lite had 4,096 tokens, Pro 8,192, Ultra 16,384, and that was it. If your hardware could handle more, it didn't matter. If it couldn't, the model could become slow or unstable.

The Python backend in version 0.9.1 loaded over 25 libraries, including LangChain, LangGraph, LanceDB, BeautifulSoup, APScheduler, and dozens of others. Each one increased startup time and memory consumption.

For 1.2.0, I reduced from **25+ dependencies to just 10**. Agent orchestration was moved to a dedicated Node.js engine, much lighter and faster to start. Libraries like LangChain, LangGraph, LanceDB, and BeautifulSoup were removed. What remains is the essentials: Kokoro-ONNX for voice, Faster-Whisper for speech recognition, SQLAlchemy for the local database, and ONNX Runtime for efficient inference.

Text-to-speech is powered by **Kokoro-ONNX**, with automatic CUDA acceleration: if you have an NVIDIA GPU, voice synthesis is processed on it. 9 languages are supported: Brazilian Portuguese, English (American and British), Spanish, Italian, French, Japanese, Chinese, and Hindi. The voice is pre-initialized so the first spoken response has no delay.

**Call mode** adds real-time audio visualization and streamed captions, an experience closer to natural conversation, all running locally.

**Extension store.** Skills installable directly from the interface: weather, web search, memory, scheduling. The architecture was designed to be simple to expand.

*MomAI v1.2.0: your data stays on your computer. Free, local, private.*
